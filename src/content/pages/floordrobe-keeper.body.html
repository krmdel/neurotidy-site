The Floordrobe Keeper is the ADHD mess type where clothes live on the floor, the chair and the exercise bike, and clean and dirty have merged into one layer. Putting clothes away is about six invisible steps your brain reads as a single wall, so they stay out, and the fix is one basket and one rule.

How to recognise it

The dresser is basically decorative. Yesterday's jeans are on the floor with your keys still in the pocket. The chair holds clothes that are too clean to wash and too worn to put away. When you finally start picking things up, you hit an invisible step three, get overwhelmed, and they go back down.

The kitchen might honestly be fine. It is the bedroom floor that is the crime scene. That is the Floordrobe Keeper, one of the four ADHD mess types.

What is actually going on

"Put the clothes away" sounds like one task. It is not. Pick it up, decide if it is clean, fold it or hang it, find the right drawer, open it, make room, place it, close it. Six or seven steps, each one a small decision, and no reward at the end except a slightly emptier floor.

ADHD brains run low on exactly that kind of multi-step, low-reward work. The steps do not get done because they never got seen as steps. The floor is not a discipline problem. It is what happens when the distance between "worn" and "stored" is too long for the brain to cross on a Tuesday night. The floordrobe guide and the laundry guide explain the mechanism with the research behind it.

The first card to use: The 5-Minute Floordrobe

One basket, one rule: clean goes back, dirty goes in the basket, and that is the whole job. No folding marathon, no sorting by type, no decisions at 11pm. Five minutes with a timer, then you stop.

The trick is that the card removes the steps instead of asking you to push through them. Clean clothes go back on the nearest shelf or hook, not into a folded stack. Dirty clothes go into one basket, not into a sorted system. The floor clears because the job got shorter, not because you got tougher.
