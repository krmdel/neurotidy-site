The Doom Piler is the ADHD mess type where every flat surface becomes a someday-pile: the chair, the desk, that one corner of the counter. The pile is not junk and it is not laziness, it is a stack of decisions your brain deferred, and the fix is to sort without deciding.

How to recognise it

There is a pile on the chair, and it is not even clothes. Mail, a charger, a plate, a receipt, the thing you meant to return. The kitchen has a permanent corner where paper goes to wait. When you "tidy", the pile moves to a different surface and keeps existing. You know roughly what is in there. You just cannot make it not exist.

If that is your home, you are a Doom Piler. It is the most common of the four ADHD mess types, and the one people feel the most shame about, because the pile is visible proof of every "later" that never came.

What is actually going on

Every object in the pile is a small open question: keep it, bin it, where does it go, who do I need to call about it. ADHD brains find open-ended small decisions expensive, and there is no deadline and no reward attached to answering them. So the item stalls in limbo, on the nearest flat surface, with the other items that stalled before it.

That is why the pile grows and why it feels impossible to start. It is not one task. It is forty tiny verdicts stacked on top of each other, and your brain reads the stack as a wall. The doom pile guide explains the mechanism in more detail, with the research it rests on.

It is not a character flaw. It is a category with nowhere to go.

The first card to use: Doom Pile Triage

Ten minutes, one pile, four boxes: Trash, Belongs elsewhere, Needs a decision, Keep here. You sort mechanically. No verdicts today, only addresses. When the timer ends you are allowed to stop, and the "needs a decision" box waits for a body double or a better day.

The rule that makes it work is sort, do not decide. Deciding is the expensive part. Sorting into boxes is cheap, and once the pile is four boxes it stops being a monument.
