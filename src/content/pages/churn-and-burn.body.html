The Churn and Burn is the ADHD mess type where you do not clean, you CLEAN: a four-hour deep-clean spree, spotless for two days, then nothing for three weeks until it is unbearable and you do it all again. All-or-nothing is the ADHD default, and the fix is not more discipline but a tiny floor so it never gets back to unbearable in the first place.

How to recognise it

The kitchen is spotless or a full biohazard, no in-between, and right now it is whichever end of the swing you are on. Storage is perfectly organized for about 48 hours after a deep clean, then it slides. You have a system for your keys that works beautifully for the two days right after you reset everything. The chair is empty because you cleared it hard a couple of days ago. Give it a week.

Clothes get folded and put away, then avalanche, then folded again, then avalanche. That is the Churn and Burn, one of the four ADHD mess types.

What is actually going on

An ADHD brain runs on interest, urgency and reward. A four-hour deep clean has all three: the mess got unbearable (urgency), the transformation is dramatic (reward), and it is a novel project rather than a chore (interest). A five-minute daily tidy has none of them. So the brain does the big one and skips the small ones, and the house cycles.

The shame after the crash makes it worse. You proved you can clean, so why can you not keep it clean? Because the state changed and the system did not. Nothing about the daily inputs moved. The closing-shift reset guide and the schedule that sticks guide explain why fixed, tiny routines beat heroic ones, with the research behind it.

The first card to use: The Bad-Day Floor and Reset Day

On a no-meds or low day, the floor is: teeth, water, one mug rinsed, trash tied, outfit on the chair. That is a 10 out of 10 day. It keeps the spiral from ever starting, because the house never reaches the point where only a four-hour spree can fix it.

The floor is deliberately tiny. It has to be reachable on your worst day, or it is not a floor, it is another thing to fail at. On good days you do the floor and then whatever else you like. On bad days the floor is the whole job and it counts.
