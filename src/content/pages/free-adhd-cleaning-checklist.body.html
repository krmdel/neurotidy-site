Why this checklist works for ADHD brains

A normal cleaning checklist says "clean the kitchen" and leaves the sizing to you. That is the exact step an ADHD brain stalls on: holding a vague, multi-part job in mind long enough to pick a first move. This checklist does the sizing for you. Every reset is 5 to 15 measured minutes, every line is one physical action, and the timer is the stop signal, so a reset ends when it rings and not when the room is perfect.

The second difference is the shaded bad-day line under each reset. "Rinse one mug for tomorrow." "Clear the path to the bed." "Wipe the sink." On a day you cannot function, that one line is the whole plan, and it still counts. What is on the page

Kitchen reset, 10 minutes

  • Music or a video on first
  • Fill the sink with hot soapy water, drop everything in
  • Bin: wrappers, packaging, anything obviously rubbish
  • Wipe one counter, the one you look at most
  • Dishwasher on, or wash until the song ends
  • Cups from other rooms back to the kitchen
  • Bad day: rinse one mug for tomorrow

Bedroom reset, 10 minutes

  • Curtains open, window open
  • Bed pulled straight, not made
  • Clothes on the chair: wearable to a hook, dirty to the basket
  • Floor path from door to bed cleared
  • Nightstand: cups out, rubbish out, the rest stays
  • Bad day: clear the path to the bed

Bathroom reset, 5 minutes, right after a shower

  • Counter cleared into one pile outside the door
  • Sink and taps, mirror, then toilet seat and rim, one cloth in that order
  • Floor spot-check: hair, bath mat, bin
  • Bad day: wipe the sink

Living room reset, 8 minutes

  • One bin bag round, rubbish only, no sorting
  • Cups and plates to the kitchen, not washed
  • Coffee table cleared, cushions back, shoes to the door
  • Bad day: cups to the kitchen

Laundry, basket to drawer

  • Load in, machine on. Wet to dryer the same day, with an alarm
  • Dry clothes into one basket, fold for one song, folded goes in the drawer now
  • Bad day: wet laundry out of the machine

Doom pile, 15 minutes, one basket

  • Four containers: bin, keep-here, other-room, decide-later
  • One object, one container, no thinking, until the timer rings
  • Bad day: bin only, ten items

Daily bare minimum

  • Rubbish that smells, out. Dishes soaking. A clear path through every room. One load of laundry moved one step. Tomorrow's mug rinsed.

How to use it without turning it into another failed system

  1. Print it and put it where the mess is. A checklist in a drawer is a checklist you will never see again.
  2. Pick one reset, the room that bothers you most when you walk in. Ignore the other five.
  3. Set the timer printed next to it. Start with the first line, not the hardest one.
  4. Stop when it rings, even mid-task. Tick what you did. The unticked lines are tomorrow's, not a failure.
  5. On a day you cannot start, do the shaded bad-day line in one room and close the sheet. That is a completed day.
